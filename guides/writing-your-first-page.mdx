Congratulations! Everyone “has a great idea” for a script, but you’re actually <u>doing</u> something about it. This guide explains the basics of writing a script in Arc Studio to help get you started.

If you want more details on the script elements we talk about below (Scene Headings, Action, Character, Dialogue, and more), check out our in-depth [Intro to Script Elements Guide](/guides/intro-to-script-elements).

Scenes start with the **Scene Heading**. Will the scene take place inside or outside? If inside, type `int.`, otherwise `ext.` Arc Studio will automatically convert it into a Scene Heading for you, which means it’s also auto-capitalized. Easy!

<Tip>
  Typing `int.` or `ext.` tells Arc Studio you’re writing a Scene Heading. The line will be auto-formatted as a scene heading.
</Tip>

![int](/images/migrated/ea348a0e2c38eca79f8721b4.gif)

Continue with the location where the scene is happening (e.g. `Dolby Theater` below) and then hit `Tab`. This will open the time of day pop-up, which is when this scene takes place. The most common times are DAY and NIGHT. If you want DAY, just hit `Return`. For another time of day, hit your keyboard’s down arrow `↓`  or type the first letter, like `N` for Night” and hit `Return`.

![scene heading](/images/migrated/38c07bd5a606cdafd7a8a210.gif)

Arc Studio has put you on a brand new Action line. You can type out your description of what’s happening. When you’re done, hit `Return` again and you will get a new Action line. However, we want Ana to start presenting the Oscar for Best Original Screenplay.

Hit `Tab` (`⇥`) to turn the **Action** element into a **Character** element. You’ll notice the cursor jump to the middle of the screen. Then type the name of the character who is going to talk.

<Tip>
  `Tab` turns empty **action** elements into **character** elements.
</Tip>

![action lines](/images/migrated/a033d8146340f60cef3cb4a3.gif)

Hit `Return` , and you now have an empty **dialogue** line beneath Ana’s name. You can now type what you want her to say. Once you’re done with her lines, hit `Return` and you go to a new empty character line. Now it’s Robert’s turn to talk. Type `robert` and his name will be automatically capitalized, then hit `Return` and write his dialogue.

![dialogue](/images/migrated/2a984f3d87ebb5a0b9a4c55e.gif)

Hit `Return` after you finish writing his lines, and you’re back to an empty character line. You could have Ana speak again, or you could switch to an action line by hitting `Tab`.

<Tip>
  `Tab` turns empty character elements into action elements.
</Tip>

![dialogue to action](/images/migrated/0c17c0e509f02cf77bc71fc1.gif)

You’ll notice that you only need to type the character’s name <u>once</u>. Arc Studio remembers the names and suggests them for you when you’re in a character element. Just click the name you want or select it with the arrow keys and hit `Return`.

What if you want to specify _how_ a character says a line? For instance, to indicate that they’re joking. You can do that with a **Parenthetical**. (Warning: Use these sparingly.) There are two easy ways to make a Parenthetical. You can either hit `Tab` after writing the character name, or you can type `(` in a dialogue line. Either way will take you to an empty parenthetical. Just type it, and then hit `Return` to automatically go to that character’s dialogue line.

![parentheticals](/images/migrated/11832126e58d623a6e611737.gif)

(Hey, it could happen!)

When you’re ready to write your next scene, just type `int.` or `ext.` in an action line and you have your next scene heading!

That’s it! As you saw, Arc Studio usually chooses the right element for you. You just have to hit `Tab` on occasion to change between Character elements and Action elements. (Once you get the hang of things, you can also [customize](/how-tos/editing-format-and-layout/how-do-change-editing-flow) which kind of element follows another kind of element in the settings.)

## One other thing…

You might have noticed that each element type had its own icon in the left margin. You can click on that icon to change the element type, adjust the formatting, and more.

![change element](/images/migrated/b7b04cada3751ad7a7fde68f.gif)
